# Plan: tightening phylloptim after 0.27.0

Written for: agents picking up work on this package. Each item names what to change, where, what evidence says it is worth doing, and what "done" is. The claims were verified against commit e898a0f (branch `collar-dry-pin-continuity`, 0.27.0) on 2026-11-02; the line counts come from [#206](https://github.com/traitecoevo/phylloptim/issues/206), measured on master at 0.26.2. Where an item says "measure first", the number quoted came from an audit agent's prototype and has to be reproduced before it is acted on.

## Rules that apply to every item

- **A change to any model goes through `inst/model-registry.yml`**, and `tests/testthat/test-registry.R` has to agree with the code afterwards. The registry is a record, not a check: nothing fails when the two drift, so the agent is the check.
- **No slowdown unless it is essential, and every claim of cost is measured interleaved.** Run `tests/cpp/bench_solve` on master and on the branch alternately, three times each, on an idle machine, and quote both; [`.claude/measurements.md`](.claude/measurements.md#benchmark-method) has the method. For plant-facing changes also time a plant stand (the seasonal stand in plant's `test-scm.R` is the near-end-heavy one).
- **Every trait has gradient infrastructure in R.** A new differentiable parameter needs its `par_*` slot before `par_PPFD`, a row in `.solve_price_fields` and `.gradient_owned_pars` if it is model-owned, a write in `.gradient_setter`, and a row in `.level_defaults` if it has a default. The `fitting` vignette's "Fitting a different collection of parameters" says why.
- **Bit-identical first.** A refactor that claims to change no number is asserted on all three goldens (`make -C tests/cpp build && ./test_golden && ./test_primitives`) before anything else is believed; a comment-only change is additionally shown bit-identical on the compiled objects. Reductions (`TF24_floor` at `lambda_o = 0` is `TF24`) are asserted bit for bit, not to a tolerance.
- **Comments carry contracts and hazards, not history.** State the reason, never the issue number. The nineteen hazards in `.claude/hazards.md` are the list; a new one is appended as 20.
- Open an issue when you pick an item up, and close it from the PR. Measurements, alternatives rejected and suite counts go in the PR's first comment, not its body ([plant-meta's commit-message rule](https://github.com/traitecoevo/plant-meta/blob/main/governance/commit-messages.md)).

## Order, and why

1. Two latent bugs (section A). Cheap, and each is a wrong number waiting for a caller.
2. The contracts CI does not hold (section B), including the length checks #206 asks for. Cheap, and they stop sections C and D from regrowing.
3. Shorter comments and NEWS (section C, #206 options 1 and 2). The largest reduction, no behaviour change, and it makes every later item's diff readable. Do it before the speed and design work, not after, so those diffs are not buried in comment churn.
4. The measured speed win (section D).
5. Split the header (section E, #206 option 3). No lines saved, but the design pass in F reads one subsystem at a time.
6. One roof for models and gradients (section F), which holds #206 options 4 and 5 and the audit's infrastructure findings. Its design is written below, with the decisions the owner agreed; the code follows its order.

**One PR per group, not per item** (decided 2026-11-02): A; B1–B4, B6 and C4; C1–C3, C5 and B5 (one commit per file for C1, still no code change, shown bit-identical as a whole); D; E; F's design; then F's steps on one PR at a time until the owner merges it (decided 2026-11-02). Each group opens one issue naming its items, and a group whose predecessor has not merged is stacked on it.

## A. Latent bugs — done in 0.27.1 (#207)

**A1. `evaluate_psi_stem` leaves `lambda_emergent_` stale.** `inst/include/phylloptim/leaf_model.hpp`, `Leaf::evaluate_psi_stem` (the stem-route evaluation at a prescribed stem potential, ~line 6358) writes every output of the point except `lambda_emergent_`, so a caller reading the shadow price after it gets the previous solve's. Every other exit of a solve writes it (`lambda_collar_for<K>()` or `lambda_for<K>()`); hazard 8 says why every path must. Done when the function writes it, `test_leaf.cpp`'s stale-state sweep covers the stem route's prescribed evaluation, and the goldens are bit-identical.

**A2. `leaf_batch()` never checks the prices.** `R/gradient-batch.R`, `leaf_batch()` (line 85) takes the model-owned prices as arguments and seats them without `.check_prices()`, which `leaf_solve()` (`R/leaf-model.R`, line 1083) calls on the same arguments. A batch built with a price its model does not own, or a missing one, fails later or not at all. Done when the batch calls the same check and `test-gradient-batch.R` asserts the same refusal `leaf_solve()` gives.

**A3. The C++ fixtures' `stem_P50`.** `tests/cpp/test_transpose.cpp:398`, `test_leaf.cpp:1727` and `:4863` hard-code `stem_P50 = 3.4287` where `leaf_traits()` defaults to 3.4 and plant's trait-built default is 2.889. Done when each fixture either reads the default or says in one line why it does not.

## F. One roof for models and gradients — designed (#222); code in the order below

This was #206's options 4 and 5, its further candidates, and the audit's findings on where a model or a gradient is bespoke rather than generic. The design below replaces the item list that stood here; the labels F1 to F11 are kept so references to them still resolve. The owner agreed the design's decisions D1 to D7 on 2026-11-02; D6 and D7 came from its review.

**Decided before the design pass** (2026-11-02, by the package owner). These are requirements, not options for the design to weigh:

- **Every curve runs on both routes**, collar and stem. Neither route is retired, and no curve is restricted to one.
- **Every curve has gradients**: through the R and C++ trait gradient, and through the derivative surface plant differentiates. The surface's restriction to `TF24` and `TF24_floor` (F5) is a gap to close, not a restriction to document.
- **The R bindings stay.** F2 is not a cut of the bound surface; where bindings are per curve or per route, they derive from the same definition as the curve or route.
- **The way there is common infrastructure, not more code per curve**: one definition of what a curve is, from which both routes, the gradients, the bindings and the registry entry follow. A design that meets the first three by adding per-curve code has missed the point.

### The design: a curve is one definition

**A curve is a specialisation `Curve<CostCurve::K>`**, all nine in `leaf_cost_curves.hpp`, holding everything about it in one place:

1. **Identity:** its name. `curve_name()` and `set_model()`'s lookup read it.
2. **Parameters:** the `par_*` slots it owns, each with its bound field name and its default. The aligned defaults (#156) live here and nowhere else in C++.
3. **Properties:** the rows of today's tables and ProfitMax's four branches. That is its benefit link (`Identity`, `Log`, `Scaled`), its objective's shape (difference, gain, normalised), whether its cost reads the upstream potential, whether its domain ends at `psi_crit`, whether its cost reads leaf temperature, whether it prepares normalisers before a search and exits on a degenerate one, whether it has a closed form, and the outputs its profit writes beyond the shared ones (`hydraulic_cost_` set to NA by the gain curves; ProfitMax's `carbon_gain_`, `hydraulic_cost_norm_` and `thermal_cost_`). Every property is stated in every specialisation, with no default: a defaulted `reads_upstream = false` would be #141's silent wrong answer again.
4. **Its cost and the cost's slopes, at `double` and on any scalar, side by side.** The `double` forms are the curve's existing expressions, moved verbatim, and the solve keeps calling them, so the goldens do not move and the registry's check that an equation's function references what it reads keeps passing. The scalar forms the surface needs read other primitives (the tables' closed-form rows where the `double` form reads `stem_curve_integral_deriv`, `transpiration_at` for the operating point's memoised flux, `vcmax_at`, `psi_crit_at`), so they cannot be the same text. They sit beside the `double` forms in the same specialisation, and a test holds each to its `double` form at `S = double`.
5. **λ, each curve's own expression**, moved verbatim. It is not derivable from the slope and the link: TF24's cancels the conductivity analytically, CF77's is its price, and `lambda_collar_for` has five shapes, so a generic λ would move `psi_stem_optima.tsv`'s `lambda_emergent` column. λ stays `double`-only, since the surface bills profit, uptake and residual flux and no λ; LeastCost's and ProfitMax's move the temperature block through `temp_deriv`.

**A concept checks each specialisation is complete**, so a missing piece is a compile error naming the piece, in place of eleven `static_assert` arms. `with_curve()` stays the one runtime switch, and the routes, the gradients and the bindings read `Curve<K>` instead of the tables.

What derives from the definition:

- **Both routes** read `Curve<K>` for cost, slope, λ and properties; neither holds per-curve code. `find_root_collar_psi()` solves the seated curve (F3), as `optimise()` already does.
- **Profit:** one evaluator per shape replaces the nine `profit_psi_stem_*` bodies (F11). The bound per-curve methods stay as one-line calls into it.
- **Trait gradient:** a model is a (curve, route) pair, for every curve on both routes, in `leaf_gradient()` and the batch. The batch's `route_seat` and R's `.gradient_route` seat the pair, which closes #219's first item. ProfitMax's re-seat between perturbations follows from its "prepares normalisers" property, not its name.
- **One gradient (F1):** `leaf_gradient()` becomes the batch with one observation on the caller's leaf, so no `Leaf` is built when `x` is given (`test-cost.R`'s count holds). `R/gradient.R` keeps argument handling and the output's shape. The bit-for-bit invariant, `rounded()` and "the reference implementation" retire from the guide. Two checks replace the replica first. `gradient_golden.tsv` gains rows for every curve on both routes, the damage layer and a prescribed point. And a finite-difference referee of the whole solve, per (curve, route, parameter) on a small grid with the step swept (hazard 15), checks the batch against the model rather than against itself: a golden generated from the batch would have recorded as correct the wiring bug only the replica caught, `LeastCost_beta` missing from `.gradient_setter`. The equality tests' other coverage stays, as tests of the batch: forced methods across interior, pinned and shut points, the single potential's `kmax` and `resistance`, `fast_stem_curve = FALSE`, and per-row errors.
- **Derivative surface (F5):** `operating_collar<K, S>` and `billed_outputs_at<K, S>` read `Curve<K>`'s scalar forms and link; the `static_assert`s and the inline TF24_floor arm go. Its scope stays what plant differentiates, the collar route and the multi-layer supply; the stem routes' gradients are the trait gradient's. It refuses the energy balance (decision D7) and bills carbon rather than the objective (decision D6). ProfitMax needs the most. On the layered supply its normalisers read TF24's wet bound, so `k_span` and `|A|max` carry that bound's rows (`wet_bound_at` exists). `|A|max` is maximised with the upstream potential at that reference, so its rows are `dA/dtheta` at its argmax plus the reference's. Its `max(-A)` fallback is a jump, to refuse at the switch or smooth under the no-clamp rule. And the collar route's prescribed evaluation and `replay_operating_point_for` must seed the normalisers, which they do not today (hazards 8 and 17). SOX and JW26 bring the jump at the light-compensation point that the guide keeps as an exception for products, and a difference of a whole run integrates such a jump (hazard 19).
- **R and the bindings (F2, F10):** one bound C++ function lists every curve's parameters (curve, slot, field, default), as `cost_curve_names()` already lists the curves. `.solve_price_fields`, `.gradient_owned_pars`, `.level_defaults`, `.leaf_trait_defaults`, `.gradient_setter`'s write, `align-curves.R` and `gradient::par_names()` read it. Generic bound methods taking the curve's name (its cost, profit and λ at a stem potential) cover every curve, LeastCost included. The per-curve methods stay bound but are frozen: no new curve adds one. A test asserts that every parameter in the definition is bound, registered and in the R list, as `test-registry.R` asserts every bound field is registered. The documents' curve counts are asserted against `length(cost_curve_names())`, matched on phrases about curves, since "eight" also counts the operating-point kinds.
- **The registry** keeps its `code:` pointers on the `double` forms, which read the members directly, so `docs.yml`'s check of what an equation reads still holds: Doxygen 1.9.8, CI's, records no member read through `l.`, so the resolver reads those from the function's body (0.27.4).

**After F, a new curve is:** one enumerator (and `n_cost_curves`), one arm in `with_curve()`, one `Curve<K>` specialisation, its registry entries, and its parameters. A parameter still costs more than a line, by design: a `par_*` slot (appended before `par_PPFD`, hazard 13) with the three `n_theta` asserts and CI's consumer, which guard the pack's size on purpose (hazard 12); a bound field; and either `set_traits` and plant's positional constructor if it is a trait, as `JS22_gamma`, `CMax_a` and `CMax_b` are (hazard 10), or a formal of `leaf_solve()` and `leaf_batch()` if it is an owned field. The R tables, `par_names()`, the tests' per-curve lists and the documents' counts derive from the definition. What is missing fails by name: the concept in C++, the bound-parameter test in R. ### Decisions (agreed by the owner, 2026-11-02)

- **D1. The closed form (F6) stays, frozen.** Its three curves carry "has a closed form" in their definitions, no new curve needs one, and it stays outside every gradient. Its three per-curve solvers stay in `closed_form.hpp`, outside the definition; frozen means no new curve adds one.
- **D2. Medlyn (F8) stays outside the curve dispatch**, as an empirical-conductance diagnostic. It cannot be a curve as written: the USO form is not a cost the solve maximises, and no λ maps onto `g1`. The registry says so, and `g1_eff`, which reads none of it, moves out of its header.

- **D5. `find_root_collar_psi()` solves the seated curve** (step 1): breaking only for a script that seats another curve and relies on getting TF24, which is #219's trap.
- **D6. The surface bills carbon, not the objective.** plant books the surface's `profit` as the leaf's carbon, which is right for TF24 and wrong for a product (SOX, JW26), a ratio (LeastCost), a normalised objective (ProfitMax) and any cost that is a price. The surface bills `A` less that part as the leaf's carbon, beside the objective that placed the point, and plant books the carbon; its own subtraction of TF24_floor's `lambda_o·E` goes (step 9).
- **D7. The surface refuses the energy balance by name**, as it refuses the thermal-damage layer: today it holds leaf temperature fixed with the energy balance on, for every curve, and says nothing. plant reaches the surface only in a gradient run (`record_leaf_outputs`, for a non-`double` scalar), so the refusal reaches only a gradient run of a TF24 strategy with `pars$use_energy_balance` non-zero, which no plant test makes (step 2).
